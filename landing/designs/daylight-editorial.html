01 TWENTY-TWO TOOLS · ONE TAB

The Studio

Open Local Desk and the first thing you notice is what is missing: the upload. No file leaves the machine — there is no spinner promising a far-away server, no progress bar pointed at somebody else’s data centre. Pages are parsed in local memory, inside the tab, on the computer already in front of you.

The studio holds twenty-two tools, arranged the way a print shop arranges its benches. Merge, split and organize pages into any order. Compress a bloated annual report until it emails cleanly. Seal a contract in a password envelope, sign it with a SHA-256 fingerprint, and let the batch queue chew through a folder while you get on with your day.

Scanned pages get the same respect. OCR runs on a Tesseract engine bundled into the app as WebAssembly, so recognition happens offline — where recognition belongs. And because the whole studio is MIT-licensed, you can read every line of it yourself.

03 CONTENT-STREAM PURGING · VERIFIED

Security

Most redaction is theatre. A black rectangle is drawn across the name — and the name is still underneath, sitting in the content stream, one copy-paste away from an incident. Local Desk does it properly: the string is removed from the content stream itself, not merely covered up.

Then the tool proves its work. The document is re-parsed and searched for any trace of what was purged, and the result is printed for the record — as in the live figure below. The same discipline runs through everything else: SHA-256 signatures for integrity, password envelopes for confidentiality, and a standing rule that nothing is transmitted, ever.
